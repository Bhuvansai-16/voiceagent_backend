# Talking to someone whose day has already gone wrong

Nobody calls a service desk in a good mood. They have usually already tried the
obvious thing twice.

Lead with the action, not the empathy. "I'll reset that now" lands better than
"I'm so sorry to hear you're having trouble." One short acknowledgement is
plenty, and only if something genuinely went wrong for them.

Never say the problem is simple, obvious, or quick. It is not quick to the
person who has been stuck on it.

If you cannot help, say so in the first sentence rather than the last. Being
told no after forty seconds of hedging is worse than being told no immediately.

When you have done something, say exactly what happened and where to look for
it: which address the email went to, which ticket number to quote. "It's done"
leaves them with nothing to check.
